Selling a House With an Unresolved Title Claim

Published: September 6, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

An unresolved title claim can raise questions about who has an interest in a property or whether the seller can transfer clear ownership. Finding the issue early gives you more time to understand it, gather records, and choose a realistic sale plan.

Review the Title Information

Start with the title commitment, deed history, court records, and any documents identified by the title company. Confirm the name of the claimant, the type of interest being asserted, and whether the claim relates to an estate, divorce, old contract, lien, or recording error.

Gather Supporting Records

Collect deeds, releases, probate papers, settlement agreements, payoff letters, canceled checks, and prior closing documents. Complete records can help the title company or attorney determine whether the claim has already been resolved but was never properly recorded.

Understand the Resolution Path

Some issues may be addressed with a corrected deed, affidavit, release, or additional signature. Others can require negotiation or a court action. Ask what must happen before closing, who needs to participate, and what timeline and costs are reasonably expected.

Plan Around the Closing Date

A buyer and title company will generally need an acceptable way to transfer ownership. Build time into the sale plan for document review and recording, and avoid promising a firm closing date until the professionals handling title confirm the required steps.

Compare an As-Is Cash Sale

A cash offer does not erase a valid title claim, but a direct buyer may offer a simpler transaction with fewer property-condition and financing requirements. Compare the offer, title-resolution responsibilities, timing, expenses, and estimated net proceeds before deciding how to sell.
