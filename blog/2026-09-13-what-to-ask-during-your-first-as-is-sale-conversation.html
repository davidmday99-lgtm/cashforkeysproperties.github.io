What to Ask During Your First As-Is Sale Conversation

Published: September 13, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

Your first conversation about selling a house as-is is a chance to explain the property and learn how the buyer works. A short question list can help you keep track of the details without trying to remember everything during the call. For homeowners in the Sarasota area or St. Louis, start with the parts of the process that affect your day-to-day plans.

What Information Should I Have Ready?

Ask which property details would be useful before a visit. You might have photos, notes about unfinished repairs, or an approximate age for major equipment. Say when a detail is unknown, and ask how to share any requested records. Keep your own copy of the information you provide.

Who Will Visit the Property?

Ask who would attend a walkthrough, what areas they want to see, and how much time to set aside. Mention pets, locked storage areas, or scheduling limits ahead of time. If another person helps you coordinate access, decide who will confirm the appointment so everyone receives the same information.

How Should We Discuss Remaining Belongings?

Explain which items you plan to take and which you would like to leave. Ask how the buyer wants to document those expectations. A garage full of boxes and a few pieces of furniture are different situations, so describe the actual rooms and items involved instead of relying on a broad phrase such as "some things will stay."

Which Dates Are Still Tentative?

Share your preferred moving window and any arrangements that are not settled yet. Ask what steps need to happen before a proposed schedule can be confirmed. Write tentative dates in your notes as tentative, especially if you still need to coordinate family help, transportation, or a place to move.

What Happens After This Conversation?

Ask whether the next step is a visit, a request for more information, or a follow-up discussion. Find out who your contact will be and when you should expect to hear back. If the buyer sends documents, set aside time to read them and ask questions about anything you do not understand before making a decision.

Keep a Short Record of the Answers

Note the date, the person you spoke with, the questions answered, and anything still open. For a Sarasota home, that might include access to a screened patio or storage shed; for a St. Louis property, it might include a basement or detached garage. Send a brief recap when details need confirmation, and keep the replies together for your next conversation.
