Selling a House With a Title Defect

Published: August 26, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

A title defect is a problem in the property records that may raise questions about legal ownership or another party's interest in a home. Missing signatures, recording errors, unreleased liens, and unresolved estate matters are common examples. Identifying the issue early can help you understand what may be required before a sale can close.

Order a Title Review Early

A title company or real estate attorney can search the public records and explain any exceptions or defects they find. Starting before you accept an offer may give you more time to locate documents, contact the appropriate parties, and avoid surprises late in the transaction.

Gather Supporting Records

Collect deeds, mortgage releases, probate documents, divorce orders, lien satisfactions, and prior title policies that relate to the property. Accurate records can help qualified professionals determine whether the problem is a clerical error or requires a more involved resolution.

Understand Possible Solutions

The appropriate solution depends on the defect. It may involve recording a corrective deed, obtaining a missing release, completing an estate process, or asking a court to clarify ownership. A local attorney and title professional can explain the available steps, costs, and likely timeline.

Plan for Disclosure and Timing

Share known title concerns with the professionals handling your sale and discuss any applicable disclosure duties. Traditional buyers and lenders may require clear, insurable title before closing, so build realistic time into the transaction and avoid guaranteeing a resolution date.

Compare an As-Is Sale

If the property also needs repairs or you want a simpler sale process, compare an as-is cash offer with the costs and uncertainty of listing. A direct buyer still needs a legally transferable interest, but a flexible closing plan may make it easier to coordinate the sale with the title work.
