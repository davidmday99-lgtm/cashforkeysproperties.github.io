How to Organize Property Notes Before an As-Is Sale

Published: September 10, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

When you have lived in a house for years, it can be hard to remember every repair, unfinished project, or small problem during one conversation. A simple set of property notes can help you describe the home clearly when exploring an as-is sale. Start with what you already know and add details as questions come up.

Use One Page for the Basics

Write the property address, your preferred contact method, and the best times to reach you. Add practical details such as whether the house is occupied, where visitors can park, and which areas are difficult to access. Keep private entry codes separate from notes you may share.

Group Observations by Room or Area

Give each main area a short heading: kitchen, bathrooms, bedrooms, basement, and exterior. Under each heading, record things you have noticed in everyday use. A note such as “the kitchen window sticks when opening” is more useful than a broad statement that the windows are bad. There is no need to inspect hard-to-reach spaces to fill out the page.

Separate Observations From Unanswered Questions

If you see a ceiling stain but do not know when it appeared, record both facts. Avoid guessing about a cause, repair cost, or installation date. Put questions in a separate section so you can ask the buyer what additional information would help.

Match Existing Records to Your Notes

Gather repair receipts, service records, or contractor estimates you already have and label them by area and date. If a receipt describes a repair to one section of the roof, keep that detail instead of calling it a full roof replacement. You can note that a record is unavailable when you do not have it.

Add Your Practical Priorities

Make a short list of what you need to discuss, such as your preferred moving window, belongings you are still sorting, or scheduling around work. Mark which dates are flexible. These notes can help you explain your situation without losing track of the property questions you also want answered.

Keep a Dated Follow-Up List

After a call or visit, write down the questions that remain, who is following up, and any expected response date. Update the same set of notes as you learn more. Before sharing documents, remove unrelated personal information and check that the recipient is the person you intend to contact.
