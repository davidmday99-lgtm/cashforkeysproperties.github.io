Selling a House With an Easement Issue

Published: August 24, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

An easement gives someone a defined right to use part of a property, often for access, utilities, or drainage. When the documents or everyday use are unclear, buyers may have questions. Organizing the facts early can help you compare a traditional listing with an as-is option.

Find the Recorded Easement

Gather your deed, title documents, survey, plat, and any separate easement agreement. Note who benefits from the easement, what area it covers, and what uses the recorded language allows. A title professional or real estate attorney can help interpret documents that are difficult to understand.

Compare the Records With Current Use

Walk the property and document where the easement is located and how it is being used today. Photos, measurements, and a current survey may help identify whether a driveway, utility line, fence, shed, or landscaping affects the easement area.

Review Maintenance Responsibilities

Some agreements address repairs, access, insurance, or shared costs, while others say very little. Keep copies of invoices and written communications related to past maintenance. Avoid making informal changes before a qualified local professional reviews the recorded rights.

Prepare for Disclosure and Title Questions

Traditional buyers and lenders may want clear documentation, especially if there is a disagreement or an improvement inside the easement area. Discuss applicable disclosure requirements and possible title solutions with appropriate local professionals before accepting a contract.

Compare an As-Is Sale

If resolving an easement issue would take too much time or expense, compare an as-is cash offer with survey costs, professional fees, carrying costs, and the uncertainty of a traditional sale. Share known conditions so potential buyers can evaluate the property accurately.
