Selling a House With a Judgment Lien

Published: August 28, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

A court judgment may become a lien against real estate and create an issue when you are ready to sell. The lien does not always make a sale impossible, but it can affect clear title, the seller's proceeds, and the closing timeline. Learning what was recorded is the right place to start.

Review the Title Report

Ask a title company or real estate attorney to identify the creditor, court case, recording information, current balance, and property connected to the judgment. Do not rely only on an old credit report or assume that every judgment attaches to the property in the same way.

Verify the Amount Owed

The payoff may include interest, fees, or credits that are not obvious from the original judgment. Request a written payoff through the appropriate professional and check its expiration date so you know what amount may need to be handled at closing.

Explore the Available Resolutions

Depending on the facts, the lien may be paid from sale proceeds, negotiated, released, or challenged if it was recorded incorrectly or has already been satisfied. A qualified attorney or title professional can explain the lawful options that apply in your location.

Plan Around Your Expected Proceeds

Estimate the mortgage payoff, judgment payoff, taxes, closing expenses, and other liens before accepting an offer. This helps you understand whether the sale is likely to produce enough proceeds and prevents surprises late in the transaction.

Compare an As-Is Sale

An as-is cash sale does not remove a valid judgment lien, but it may simplify repairs, showings, and buyer financing while the title work moves forward. Compare the net proceeds, conditions, and realistic timing of every option before you choose how to sell.
