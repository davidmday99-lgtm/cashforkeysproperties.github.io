Selling a House With an Unrecorded Deed

Published: September 7, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

An unrecorded deed can create uncertainty when public records do not reflect the ownership transfer you expected. Addressing the gap early can help you understand what documentation is needed and whether the property can move toward closing.

Confirm What the County Records Show

Start by checking the recorder or clerk records for the most recent deed on file. Compare the recorded owner, legal description, parcel number, and transfer history with the deed in your possession to identify exactly where the chain of title differs.

Locate the Original Documents

Gather the signed deed, closing statement, title policy, purchase agreement, probate papers, and any correspondence from the earlier transfer. A title company or real estate attorney can review whether the deed was properly signed, notarized, delivered, and eligible to be recorded.

Ask How the Record Can Be Corrected

The solution depends on why the deed was not recorded and whether the people who signed it are available. The next step might involve recording the original, preparing a corrective deed, obtaining an affidavit, or completing a legal process. Get professional guidance before changing or filing documents.

Allow Time Before Setting a Closing Date

A buyer generally expects the seller to provide marketable title. Recording offices, title reviewers, former owners, heirs, or courts may affect the timeline, so avoid committing to a firm closing date until the title requirements and responsible parties are clear.

Compare an As-Is Cash Sale

A cash buyer cannot bypass valid ownership requirements, but a direct as-is sale may reduce financing and property-condition hurdles once the title issue is resolved. Compare the offer, expected costs, responsibilities, timing, and estimated proceeds with your other options.
