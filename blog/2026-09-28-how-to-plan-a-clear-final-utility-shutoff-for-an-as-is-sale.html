How to Plan a Clear Final Utility Shutoff for an As-Is Sale

Published: September 28, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

Final utility details are easy to overlook when you are preparing an as-is home sale. For a Sarasota-area or St. Louis property, a short plan can help keep electricity, water, gas, and other services available through the right point in the handover.

Start With the Agreement and Closing Schedule

Review the agreed possession date and ask the title company, attorney, or designated transaction contact when utilities should remain active. Avoid scheduling a shutoff based only on an expected date; a clear confirmation helps prevent an accidental interruption before the handover is complete.

List Every Service in One Place

Make a simple list of electric, water, gas, internet, trash, security, pool, or other services connected to the property. Include account numbers, provider contact details, and whether the service is automatic or requires a call. This makes final calls easier to track.

Arrange Final Readings When Needed

If a provider offers a final meter reading, request it for the confirmed transition date. Save the confirmation number and a copy of any final bill. A photo of the meter on the handover day can also be useful for your own records when appropriate.

Keep Essential Service On Until the Right Time

In many situations, utilities need to stay on until closing or possession so the home remains accessible and basic systems can be checked. Do not make changes that conflict with the sale documents. When in doubt, get direction from the appropriate transaction professional.

Share Only Practical Information

If you provide utility notes, keep them factual: provider names, account-transfer instructions, trash schedules, or the location of a meter. Do not include passwords, payment details, or personal account information. Remove sensitive documents before leaving the property.

Confirm Completion and Keep Your Records

After the handover, verify any scheduled transfer or shutoff and retain confirmations with your closing paperwork. This small final step helps you close out the property smoothly while keeping your records organized.
