Selling a House With an HOA Lien

Published: August 30, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

Unpaid homeowners association dues, assessments, fines, interest, or collection costs may lead an HOA to record a lien against a property. That lien can affect clear title and closing, but reviewing the account early can help you understand the balance and compare ways to move forward.

Confirm the Lien and Account Balance

Ask a title company or real estate attorney to verify what was recorded and request a current account ledger from the association or its management company. Compare dues, special assessments, late fees, legal charges, and payments so you can identify missing credits or unfamiliar amounts.

Review the Governing Documents

The declaration, bylaws, collection policy, and applicable law may explain how charges accrue and what steps the association can take. Because lien rights and deadlines vary by location, consult a qualified professional before assuming a charge is valid, negotiable, or expired.

Request a Written Payoff

Ask the appropriate closing professional to obtain a dated payoff statement that includes every amount required to release the lien. Confirm how long the figure is valid, whether new monthly charges will be added, and who will provide and record the release after payment.

Plan for Closing Costs and Timing

An HOA balance may be paid before closing, resolved from sale proceeds, disputed, or negotiated depending on the facts. Also ask about resale certificates, transfer fees, association approval, and other documents that could affect the closing schedule or your net proceeds.

Compare an As-Is Sale

An as-is cash sale does not remove a valid HOA lien, but it may reduce the separate work of repairs, showings, and buyer financing while the account is resolved. Compare written offers by net proceeds, contingencies, and realistic timing before choosing a sale path.
