How to Review an As-Is Home Offer

Published: September 17, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

Receiving an offer can feel like a turning point, but the price is only one part of the decision. Before responding, read the entire written offer and make a short list of anything that is unclear. Whether the property is in the Sarasota area or St. Louis, a careful review can help you understand what is proposed and what still needs to be discussed.

Confirm the Property and People

Start with the basics. Check the property address, the names of the parties, and the contact information shown in the offer. If the home is owned by more than one person, held in a trust, or connected to an estate, make sure the ownership details you provided are reflected accurately. Flag a spelling error or missing person instead of assuming it can be corrected later.

Read the Price Alongside the Terms

Write down the offered price, then note any conditions, credits, or costs described in the document. Look for language about inspections, access, title work, and who is expected to handle particular expenses. An as-is offer may address repairs differently from a traditional sale, so rely on the written terms rather than a general impression from an earlier conversation.

Trace the Proposed Timeline

Identify each date or time period in the offer, including any response deadline and proposed closing date. Consider whether the schedule gives you enough time to coordinate with other owners, collect documents, move belongings, and make plans for the property. Ask how a date is calculated if the wording uses business days or depends on another event.

Clarify What Stays With the Property

Review any section covering appliances, fixtures, furniture, or other belongings. Make a room-by-room note of items you plan to remove and items you expect to leave. If a garage, shed, attic, or outdoor area contains belongings, mention it specifically. Written clarity can prevent different assumptions about what the property will contain at closing.

List Every Open Question

Keep questions in one place and connect each one to the relevant section of the offer. You might ask what a term means, whether another visit is anticipated, or which document is needed next. Avoid filling in missing details yourself. For legal, tax, or financial questions, consider speaking with an appropriately qualified professional who can review your situation.

Keep the Final Version Together

If terms change, make sure you receive an updated written version and review the changes before responding. Save the offer, related messages, and any notes about agreed next steps in one folder. A complete record makes it easier to follow the process and return to the exact language if another question comes up.
