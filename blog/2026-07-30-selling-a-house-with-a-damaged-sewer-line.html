Selling a House With a Damaged Sewer Line

Published: July 30, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

A damaged sewer line can turn a home sale into a stressful decision. Slow drains, backups, odors, or wet areas in the yard may concern buyers, inspectors, and lenders, but homeowners can gather the facts and compare repair costs with an as-is sale.

Confirm the Source of the Problem

Similar symptoms can come from a localized clog, damaged interior plumbing, or a problem in the main sewer line. A licensed plumber can evaluate the system, and a camera inspection may show cracks, root intrusion, corrosion, or a collapsed section without unnecessary digging.

Understand Who Is Responsible

Responsibility may depend on where the damage is located and local utility rules. Ask the sewer authority or municipality where the homeowner's responsibility ends, then review any available service-line coverage before approving work.

Compare Complete Repair Estimates

Traditional excavation is not always the only approach. Depending on the pipe and site, a contractor may discuss lining, bursting, or replacing a limited section. Make sure estimates account for permits, excavation, landscaping, pavement, cleanup, and any required interior work.

Disclose Known Conditions

Share known problems, inspection results, and completed repair records accurately with prospective buyers. Disclosure requirements vary, so a local real estate professional or attorney can explain what should be provided and help reduce surprises during inspections or closing.

Consider an As-Is Sale

If a sewer repair does not fit the seller's budget or timeline, an as-is cash sale may provide another route. Compare the offer with the expected repair expense, buyer concessions, financing delays, restoration costs, and the time required for a traditional listing.
