How to Prepare for an As-Is Home Closing

Published: September 18, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

Once you have agreed to an as-is home sale, a simple closing checklist can help you keep the remaining details in order. The exact process depends on the written agreement and the professionals handling the transaction, but sellers in the Sarasota area and St. Louis can prepare by confirming documents, property access, belongings, and important dates.

Confirm the Closing Details in Writing

Review the scheduled date, signing arrangements, and location or remote-signing instructions. Ask who will contact you with final directions and how you should verify that a message is legitimate. If you expect to be traveling or unavailable, raise that issue early so the closing professional can explain the available options.

Gather Property and Ownership Documents

Keep your identification and any documents requested by the title or closing professional together. These may relate to ownership, an estate or trust, an existing loan, prior work on the property, or other facts specific to the home. Respond promptly when something is requested, and ask for clarification instead of sending sensitive information through an unfamiliar channel.

Finish Your Belongings Plan

Use the written agreement to confirm what you will remove and what may remain. Walk through bedrooms, closets, cabinets, the garage, sheds, and outdoor areas so overlooked items do not create a last-minute problem. If circumstances have changed since the offer was accepted, discuss them before closing rather than assuming the plan can change automatically.

Coordinate Keys and Property Access

Collect house keys, mailbox keys, gate controls, garage remotes, and any written access instructions. Confirm when and how they should be transferred. If another person has a key, a tenant occupies the property, or a service provider needs access, make a note and tell the appropriate contact so everyone has the same expectations.

Handle Utilities With the Timeline in Mind

Ask when utilities should be transferred or discontinued before scheduling a shutoff. Water, electricity, or another service may need to remain active through a final visit or closing. Keep confirmation numbers for any changes and update your mailing address with the providers that need it.

Review Final Information Carefully

Read closing documents and figures when they are provided, and report a name, address, date, or amount that appears incorrect. Direct legal, tax, and financial questions to appropriately qualified professionals. Be cautious with payment or wiring instructions: independently verify them through a trusted phone number before acting on a message that requests money or account information.
