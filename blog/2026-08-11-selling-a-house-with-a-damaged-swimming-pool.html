Selling a House With a Damaged Swimming Pool

Published: August 11, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

Cracked surfaces, leaking plumbing, broken equipment, or cloudy water can turn a swimming pool from a selling feature into a concern. Understanding the condition and likely cost can help you decide whether to repair the pool or sell the property as-is.

Make the Area Safe

Secure gates, fencing, covers, and damaged decking to reduce immediate hazards. If electrical equipment, drains, or structural components appear unsafe, keep people away and contact a qualified pool professional before attempting repairs.

Get a Professional Evaluation

A pool inspector or licensed contractor can assess the shell, liner, plumbing, pump, filter, heater, electrical system, and surrounding deck. Ask for written findings so you can distinguish routine maintenance from leaks, structural movement, or equipment that needs replacement.

Compare Repair and Removal Costs

Request itemized estimates for repairs and, when appropriate, partial or complete pool removal. Costs can vary based on pool type, access, permits, drainage, soil conditions, decking, landscaping, and local rules for filling or removing a pool.

Keep Records and Disclose Known Problems

Save inspection reports, photographs, service records, permits, warranties, and contractor estimates. Disclosure requirements vary by location, so ask a local real estate professional or attorney how known pool defects, leaks, or unpermitted work should be shared with buyers.

Consider an As-Is Sale

If repairs or removal would strain your budget or delay your plans, compare an as-is cash offer with contractor costs, buyer concessions, maintenance, insurance, utilities, and the uncertainty of a traditional sale.
