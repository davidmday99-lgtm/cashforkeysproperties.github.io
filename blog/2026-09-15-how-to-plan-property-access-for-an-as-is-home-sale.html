How to Plan Property Access for an As-Is Home Sale

Published: September 15, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

Arranging a property visit is easier when everyone knows when to arrive, who will meet them, and which areas they can see. Whether you live at the house or coordinate from another city, a short access plan can help keep an as-is sale conversation moving. Here are practical details to work through for a Sarasota-area or St. Louis home.

Offer Times You Can Actually Confirm

Start with two or three possible visit windows that fit your schedule. Include time for someone to arrive and walk through the property without rushing. If a relative or helper will meet the visitor, check their availability before offering a time. Treat the appointment as tentative until everyone has confirmed the date and arrival window.

Agree on Who Will Meet the Visitor

Write down the visitor's name, the person meeting them, and a contact number for arrival changes. If you will be away, choose a helper who knows the property and understands what has been arranged. When other people live at the house, coordinate the visit with them first rather than assuming that an available calendar slot means the home is ready for a visit.

Describe the Entrance and Parking

A brief note can prevent confusion at the curb. Mention which driveway to use, where to park, and whether the meeting point is the front porch or another entrance. For a Sarasota home, you might clarify how to reach a screened entry; for a St. Louis property, you might explain where a rear garage sits. Share entry codes privately with the intended visitor only when needed, and keep them out of public listings and photos.

Identify Areas That Need Separate Arrangements

A detached shed, locked garage, or storage room may need a different key or someone else's help to open. Make a short list of those areas before the appointment. Tell the visitor if an area will be unavailable so they can plan any follow-up questions. If a space appears unsafe to enter, describe the concern and leave access to an appropriate professional.

Make a Plan for Pets and Personal Items

Arrange a comfortable place for pets during the visit and let the person meeting the visitor know the plan. Put personal paperwork and small valuables somewhere secure. You do not need to turn the house into a showroom to discuss its current condition, but clear instructions and a manageable walking path can help the appointment go more smoothly.

Confirm What Happens After the Visit

Before everyone leaves, ask whether another visit is being proposed and which areas it would involve. Record any agreed next step and coordinate a new time separately. If a helper opened the property, ask them to confirm that doors and gates are secured afterward. Keeping access arrangements specific helps you stay organized while you consider your next steps.
