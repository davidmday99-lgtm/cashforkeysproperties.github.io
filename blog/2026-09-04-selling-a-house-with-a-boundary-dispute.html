Selling a House With a Boundary Dispute

Published: September 4, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

A disagreement about a fence, driveway, shed, or property line can complicate a home sale. Buyers and title companies want to understand exactly what is being transferred, so documenting the issue early can help prevent delays.

Gather the Property Records

Start with the deed, legal description, title policy, prior surveys, permits, and any written agreements with neighboring owners. These records can help show whether the concern involves an uncertain boundary, an encroachment, an easement, or simply a misplaced improvement.

Consider an Updated Survey

A licensed surveyor can locate the recorded boundary and identify visible improvements near it. Ask the closing or title professional what type of survey is appropriate before ordering one, since requirements and accepted survey standards vary.

Document Communication Carefully

Keep copies of letters, emails, photographs, estimates, and proposed solutions. Avoid making promises about ownership or moving shared improvements until the relevant records have been reviewed. Clear documentation gives the professionals handling the sale a better picture of the situation.

Review Possible Solutions

Depending on the facts, the parties may consider a boundary agreement, easement, lot-line adjustment, removal of an encroachment, or another documented resolution. A real estate attorney, surveyor, and title company can explain which options may work locally and how long they could take.

Compare an As-Is Cash Sale

An as-is cash sale does not eliminate a valid boundary issue, but it may reduce repair requests and financing complications. Compare the offer, expected resolution costs, closing conditions, timing, and net proceeds so you understand the practical tradeoffs.
