Selling a House With a Mechanic's Lien

Published: August 29, 2026By: Cash For Keys Properties

A contractor, subcontractor, or supplier may record a mechanic's lien when payment for property improvements is disputed. The claim can affect clear title and delay a sale, but identifying the lien early gives you more time to understand the balance and plan for closing.

Confirm What Was Recorded

Ask a title company or real estate attorney to review the lien's claimant, recording date, amount, property description, and current status. Requirements and deadlines vary by location, so do not assume that an old or unfamiliar claim is automatically valid or expired.

Gather the Project Records

Collect contracts, invoices, receipts, change orders, canceled checks, releases, and messages related to the work. These records can help a qualified professional determine whether the claimed amount matches the agreement and whether any payments or disputes were overlooked.

Request a Written Payoff or Release

If the claim is valid, ask the appropriate professional to obtain a current payoff and written instructions for releasing the lien. Confirm whether interest, legal costs, or recording fees apply and how long the payoff figure remains effective.

Coordinate the Resolution Before Closing

A lien may be paid from sale proceeds, settled, bonded off, released, or challenged depending on local law and the facts. Your title company or attorney can explain which path may produce insurable title and what documents must be recorded.

Compare an As-Is Sale

An as-is cash sale does not erase a valid mechanic's lien, but it may reduce the separate burden of repairs, showings, and buyer financing while the title issue is addressed. Compare each option's net proceeds, conditions, and realistic closing timeline before deciding how to sell.
